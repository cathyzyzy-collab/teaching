Who Gets Paid?

A corporation's value is divided among the people who put money into it. Lenders hold fixed claims: a legal promise to be repaid with interest. Shareholders hold the residual claim: no repayment promise, but ownership of whatever is left. Think of an hourglass: the firm's value pours down and fills each claimant's cup in order.

✓What to notice

1. Shareholders are the residual claimants, and the owners

Debt is a legal obligation: the firm promises to repay it with interest, so lenders hold a fixed claim and are paid first. Equity has no repayment promise but ownership in the firm, a share in the company's future. Shareholders get only what is left, with no cap. That makes common equity the riskiest claim, so investors demand the highest expected return on it.

2. Limited liability puts a floor at $0

A corporation's shareholders can lose what they invested but never more: their payoff stops at $0 even when the firm owes more than it is worth. Proprietors and partners have unlimited liability and would have to cover the shortfall personally. In the BP story, the stock lost more than half its value, but the bill for the spill went to the company, not to shareholders' personal wealth.

3. The goal, and the conflicts it creates

The main financial goal is shareholder wealth maximization: making the residual claim as valuable as possible, ideally its long-run intrinsic value. But with debt outstanding, shareholders can gain from risk at bondholders' expense (section 3), even when it shrinks the firm. Lenders respond with covenants and higher interest rates, and the same tension raises the question in the opening story: whose interests should the corporation serve?

Simplifying assumptions: one payout date; strict priority (each class is paid in full before the next gets anything); no bankruptcy or legal costs; amounts owed include any interest due; expected values ignore the time value of money and investors' attitudes toward risk. Real bankruptcies often deviate from strict priority, and legal costs shrink what everyone receives.
