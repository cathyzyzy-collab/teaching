Yield Curve Time Machine

The yield curve plots the yields on U.S. Treasury securities against their maturities for a single month: the term structure of interest rates. Treasuries have essentially no default risk, so the shape of the curve reflects expected future short-term rates and inflation, plus a maturity risk premium. Move through the real data since 1990 and watch the shape change.

3What to notice

    Each value is the monthly average of daily constant-maturity yields. Maturities are evenly spaced on the chart, not drawn to scale, so the short end is readable. Where a maturity was not published for a month (the 1-month bill before August 2001, the 20-year bond before October 1993) the curve is drawn through the maturities that were published. The label uses the 10-year minus 2-year spread: below −0.05 points is “Inverted”, −0.05 to +0.25 is “Flat”, and above +0.25 is “Normal”.
