How it's calculated

APR = rate per period × periods per year (m). Effective annual rate EAR = (1 + APR/m)m − 1.

Sources and notes

Stock-return default in story 1: the S&P 500's compound (geometric) average of 10.02% a year with dividends reinvested, 1928–2025, from the same Damodaran dataset.

Powerball: “Ticket in California wins world record $2.04 billion Powerball jackpot,” November 8, 2022 (powerball.com). Annuity: 30 graduated payments over 29 years, rising 5% a year. Taxes ignored.

Payday loan default: the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau reported finance charges of “$15 or $20 for each $100 borrowed” over a typical two-week term, an APR of 391% to 521% (“Consumer Financial Protection Bureau examines payday lending,” January 19, 2012).

Credit card default: 22.36%, the average rate on credit-card accounts assessed interest at U.S. commercial banks, August 2026 (Federal Reserve G.19 via FRED, series TERMCBCCINTNS).

The buy-now-pay-later late fee, the rent-to-own deal and the credit-card balances are illustrative examples, not quotes from any company. Fees, terms and legal limits vary by lender and by state. An effective annual rate assumes the same deal is repeated all year.

Unless a story says otherwise, rates are annual with annual compounding (P/Y = C/Y = 1). Sign convention as in class: money paid out is negative, money received is positive. Everything is calculated in your browser; nothing is stored or sent.
