Beta and the Security Market Line

A diversified investor cares about one kind of risk: how much a stock moves with the market. Beta (β) measures it, and CAPM turns beta into a required return. Everything here uses real monthly returns for ten well-known U.S. stocks over the last five years.

1Beta from a scatter plot

Each dot is one month: the market's return across, the stock's return up. The best-fit line through the dots is the stock's characteristic line, and its slope is beta: how many percentage points the stock moved, on average, for each 1-point move in the market. Pick two stocks to compare.

2The Security Market Line

CAPM turns beta into a required return. Plot required return against beta and you get a straight line, the SML. It starts at the risk-free rate (β = 0), passes through the market (β = 1), and its slope is the market risk premium, rM − rRF.

Predict first.

ri = rRF + βi (rM − rRF)

3Portfolio beta

A portfolio's beta is the weighted average of its stocks' betas. Move the weights and the portfolio slides along the SML between the two stocks. The rRF and market risk premium come from section 2.
